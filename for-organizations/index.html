MyDopa for Organizations

Structured learning tells people what to do differently. A daily practice gives them evidence that they did.

Most organizational development programs are designed around the training event — the workshop, the cohort, the course. What happens in the six weeks after the event is, for most participants, entirely unstructured and invisible. MyDopa addresses that period.

The transfer gap

The research on training transfer points to what happens after.

The field of transfer-of-training research is consistent on one point: learning events rarely fail because of the content delivered during the event. They fail because individuals return to daily work without a low-friction structure for noticing, applying, and preserving the moments when the new skill or framework was actually used.

Most programs track whether the training occurred — attendance, completion, a satisfaction survey administered the same day. Almost none track whether anything changed in the six weeks that followed. The measurement captures the event. The application period — where transfer either happens or doesn't — is largely unmeasured and unsupported.

MyDopa is built for that period.

How it works

A private daily practice: two minutes, one moment, every day.

The MyHGY™ Method asks individuals to find — and preserve — one specific moment from their own recent experience: something they noticed, handled, or applied differently than they would have before. Not what they intended to do. What they actually did.

The practice is private. MyDopa records are not visible to managers, not reported to HR, and not connected to performance review systems. The record belongs to the individual. The value is in the individual's growing, cumulative body of evidence about their own development — not in the organization's visibility into it.

Over time, the accumulated record makes something visible that most development programs leave invisible: the real-work evidence that learning was used.

Complement, not replace

MyDopa™ works alongside the programs you already have.

MyDopa is not a training platform. It is a daily practice tool built to support individuals in applying what formal development programs have already introduced — by making that application visible, specific, and cumulative.

MyDopa for organizations, explained

What is MyDopa for Organizations?

MyDopa for Organizations provides individuals within structured learning programs a private daily practice layer — a personal companion to formal development sessions. Where a training program or workshop focuses on knowledge transfer during a scheduled event, MyDopa supports the individual application of that learning between sessions, in real work, in real situations.

Why do training programs often fail to produce lasting change?

The research on training transfer is consistent: most transfer failure happens not during the training event but in the weeks that follow, when individuals return to the demands of daily work without a structured mechanism for applying, noticing, and reinforcing what they learned. The training event itself is rarely the limiting factor.

What happens between formal development sessions?

In most programs, the period between sessions is unstructured — individuals are expected to apply learning independently, without feedback or documentation. For most people, this means that application is sporadic and invisible. MyDopa provides a private, frictionless daily structure for capturing specific moments of application — the individual's own real-work evidence that learning is being used.

Who uses MyDopa in an organizational context?

Individual participants in development programs. MyDopa is a personal tool — not a reporting system for managers, not a completion tracker, and not connected to performance review processes. Each person's record is their own. The value is in the individual's growing body of evidence about their own development — not in the organization's visibility into that record.

How does MyDopa complement existing L&D programs?

MyDopa does not replace facilitated learning. It provides the between-session layer that most programs leave unaddressed — a daily practice of noticing and preserving specific moments of application. This makes what happens after the training visible to the individual, even when it remains invisible to the organization's measurement systems.

What is the MyHGY™ Method in a professional context?

The same practice as in personal development, applied to the professional environment. A two-minute daily entry: one specific moment from today that represents growth, capability, or effective application of a skill. The specificity is what produces usable evidence — not what you intended to do differently, but what you actually did differently.

Is MyDopa a reporting tool for managers?

No. MyDopa records are private to the individual. There is no organizational dashboard, no manager view, no aggregate reporting. The tool's value is in giving individuals their own evidence of their own development — not in giving organizations a monitoring mechanism.

How does a daily practice reinforce formal organizational learning?

Formal learning introduces concepts and frameworks. A daily practice converts those frameworks into personal evidence by prompting individuals to find — and keep — real instances of application from their own work. The record that accumulates is not a reflection of the training content; it's a reflection of the individual's actual experience applying it.
