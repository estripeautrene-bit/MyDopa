## 2. Governing Definition of the Method

The Have a Great Yesterday Method™ is a practical approach to personal growth built around a simple idea: people become clearer, more accurate, and more self-confident when they learn to pay attention to what is actually happening in their lives, preserve that evidence, and return to the practice consistently.

Awareness and focus come first. A person learns to observe the present more deliberately, distinguish lived reality from uncontrolled thought and prediction, notice meaningful moments as they happen, preserve them, and return to the process often enough for those moments to accumulate into visible evidence.

That evidence matters because people build stories about themselves from experience. Repeatedly preserving real lived experience helps create a more accurate picture of the self. Over time, persistent small practices create continuity, continuity makes progress visible, and visible progress supports clarity, accuracy, and self-confidence.

The Method therefore sits at the intersection of presence, evidence, self-image, behavior, repetition, continuity, and personal development.

## 3. The Core Problem the Method Addresses

A central personal and conceptual insight behind HGY is this sequence:

**Uncontrolled thoughts → feelings and emotions → an inaccurate version of reality, often weighted by negativity bias.**

The problem begins when thoughts run ahead of lived reality. They predict, rehearse, interpret, judge, and create imagined scenarios. Those thoughts generate feelings. The feelings can then be experienced as if they were proof of what is actually happening.

Negativity bias can amplify the darker interpretation. Energy is then spent responding to a story rather than to the evidence of the present moment.

The Method introduces a different sequence:

**Present reality → awareness → noticing → capture → evidence → more accurate interpretation → clearer self-image → grounded behavior → clarity, accuracy, and self-confidence.**

The aim is greater accuracy.

## 4. The Core Thesis

The core thesis of HGY is:

**Personal development becomes durable when it is practiced continuously through small, organized, repeated actions that keep a person connected to present reality and to visible evidence of their own life and progress.**

The Method treats personal growth as a practice rather than an occasional intellectual event.

Books, podcasts, courses, conferences, therapy, coaching, and moments of insight can all create value. The Method focuses on what happens between those moments: the repeated daily work through which insight becomes lived behavior.

The Method’s distinctive promise is that short, conscious, repeated effort can build clarity, accuracy, and self-confidence from the inside.

## 5. Persistent Small Practices

The phrase **powered by persistent small practices** describes the mechanism of the Method.

“Persistent” means the practice is returned to again and again over time. The emphasis is continuity, durability, and re-engagement.

“Small” means the practice is intentionally light enough to be repeatable. The Method is designed around actions that can fit inside ordinary life rather than requiring heroic motivation.

“Practices” means the behavior is intentional, structured, and connected to a larger purpose. The Method is not simply about frequency. It is about repeated actions that form part of an organized process of inner development.

Persistent small practices allow awareness, evidence, and self-image to work together over time.

## 6. Awareness and Focus

Awareness is the doorway into the Method.

A person can live through good moments without consciously registering them. René’s own experience was that many meaningful moments were felt briefly and then disappeared from attention. Awareness changed when he began deliberately focusing on what was actually happening.

Focus gives awareness direction. It allows a person to notice a real moment long enough for it to register.

HGY therefore begins from lived experience in the present: a conversation, a laugh, an unexpected connection, a good piece of work, a moment of gratitude, an act of courage, a small follow-through, a meaningful interaction, or any other real event that matters.

This creates the first shift from automatic thought toward present evidence.

## 7. Notice, Capture, Interpret, Accumulate, Continue

An earlier working sequence for the Method was:

**Notice → Capture → Interpret → Accumulate → Continue**

Awareness and focus now sit in front of this sequence as the doorway that makes noticing possible.

A useful current expression of the full logic is:

**Awareness → Notice → Capture → Interpret → Accumulate → Continue**

This is a practical explanatory framework rather than a formally locked step model. It remains useful because it maps the Method’s core behavior.

### Awareness
Direct attention toward what is actually happening.

### Notice
Recognize the lived moment.

### Capture
Preserve it before it disappears from attention and memory.

### Interpret
Use the evidence to form a more accurate understanding of the day, the self, and reality.

### Accumulate
Allow many small moments to build into a visible body of evidence.

### Continue
Return to the practice. Continuity is what allows the evidence to gain meaning over time.

## 8. Evidence

Evidence is central to HGY.

A good moment that is briefly experienced and then forgotten has limited ability to influence self-perception. A preserved moment can be revisited, recognized as part of a pattern, and integrated into the story a person tells about themselves.

HGY uses real lived moments as evidence. The evidence is generated by the person’s own life.

This is the foundation behind the broader MyDopa phrase:

**Confidence comes from evidence.**

Self-confidence in the HGY framework grows from accumulated proof: actions taken, moments noticed, difficult things handled, relationships maintained, projects advanced, promises kept, good experiences preserved, and repeated behavior that becomes visible over time.

## 9. Self-Image

Self-image is a major mechanism inside the Method.

People build beliefs about who they are from past experiences, perceived successes and failures, feedback, repeated behavior, and remembered stories about themselves.

HGY adds a practical mechanism for influencing that process: deliberately preserving a more representative record of lived experience.

A person who repeatedly sees evidence of showing up, contributing, caring, following through, learning, recovering, acting with courage, or making progress gains material from which a more accurate self-image can form.

The Method therefore connects evidence to identity without relying on empty affirmation.

## 10. Continuity and Consistency

Continuity is what gives the Method power over time.

A single captured moment has value. A continuous record creates pattern, perspective, and visibility.

Consistency is best understood in HGY as behavior. It is the repeated act of returning to an organized process of personal development.

The Method does not define success as perfect uninterrupted streaks. When continuity breaks, the governing behavior is to return.

The important behavior is: **show up again.**

The deeper doctrine is therefore:

**Committed daily practice of showing up; when continuity breaks, return with renewed effort.**

## 11. Compounding and Visible Progress

Small actions become meaningful when they accumulate.

The Method depends on an important feature of human change: the value of a repeated behavior is often difficult to see on any single day. Continuity allows the pattern to become visible.

This creates one of the strongest bridges between HGY and the broader MyDopa philosophy of visible progress.

The person begins to see repeated evidence, recurring themes, patterns of behavior, growth over time, changes in interpretation, stronger follow-through, and an evolving self-image.

The progress becomes visible.

Visibility matters because invisible progress is easy to discount. Visible progress becomes evidence.

## 12. Clarity, Accuracy, and Self-Confidence

### Clarity

Clarity is seeing the situation more cleanly.

It grows when attention returns to actual events and the person becomes less dominated by uncontrolled predictions, mental stories, and emotional interpretations.

### Accuracy

Accuracy is the ability to evaluate life and oneself using evidence that more closely reflects what actually happened.

Negativity bias can distort memory and interpretation. HGY creates a counterweight by preserving lived moments that would otherwise disappear.

Accuracy is therefore a central promise of the Method.

### Self-Confidence

Self-confidence emerges from repeated evidence.

The Method treats confidence as something built.

As clarity and accuracy improve, a person has a better basis for deciding where to act, where to invest energy, what they can trust about themselves, and what their lived history actually demonstrates.

A core HGY idea is:

**Accuracy, clarity and self-confidence cannot be bought. They are built. This system builds them with short conscious effort.**

This is an important explanatory statement, though it is not locked word-for-word in the same way as the canonical name and locked principle.

## 13. The Daily Practice

The personal practice that helped generate HGY developed through morning and evening bookends and then expanded into real-time capture during the day.

### Morning bookend

René began writing ten things he was grateful for in the morning.

The first line in his personal practice became:

**grateful for this beautiful day and happy to be alive**

The remaining items commonly included family, health, the ability to exercise, having a roof, food, and other real parts of life.

The value was behavioral as much as reflective. It allowed the day to begin with action and attention.

### Evening bookend

Later, René adopted a nightly Three Good Things practice.

The evening practice created a deliberate return to the day and a record of meaningful events.

### Real-time capture

The breakthrough came from realizing that many meaningful moments disappeared before evening.

Examples include a dog greeting him, seeing his mother unexpectedly and receiving her smile, laughing with a daughter, connecting with colleagues, enjoying a good coffee, eating an excellent croissant, advancing a project, having a useful conversation, or noticing a small personal win.

Real-time capture allowed these fleeting moments to become preserved evidence.

The practice evolved from bookends into all-day awareness and capture.

## 15. Negativity Bias

Negativity bias is important to HGY because negative events and interpretations can receive disproportionate attention and memory.

The Method uses consciously preserved lived evidence as a counterweight.

A useful approved metaphor is a **fulcrum/counterweight**: accumulated real positive evidence helps balance the natural pull of negativity bias.

Scientific language must remain careful. HGY should not claim that it eliminates negativity bias or clinically treats distorted thinking.

The defensible claim is that intentionally noticing and preserving real experiences can provide additional evidence against which negative interpretations can be evaluated.

## 16. The Cultural-Gap Thesis

A major HGY thesis is that society already understands continuous practice in many external domains: fitness, golf, tennis, music, language learning, rehabilitation, surgery, and professional skill development.

People understand that improvement in these areas comes from repeated practice over time.

Personal development is often consumed more episodically: a book, a podcast, a seminar, a conference, a coaching session, a therapy session, or a course.

HGY argues that personal development deserves the same relationship to practice.

The Method is meant to help establish a simple cultural idea:

**personal growth is something a person practices continuously.**

This thesis is a major content territory for the HGY / IP lane and should be explored through research, history, professional commentary, and practical explanation.

## 17. Consistency as Behavior

Consistency should be treated as behavior rather than as a personality trait or motivational slogan.

Frequency alone is insufficient.

A person can repeat an action often without improving. The quality, structure, direction, and relationship to the process matter.

A golfer can play frequently without practicing deliberately. A professional can repeat tasks without learning. A person can “be consistent” in behaviors that conflict with their stated goals.

In HGY, consistency means:

**returning repeatedly to a structured process that supports the person’s intended development.**

This is why the Method emphasizes an organized practice rather than repetition for its own sake.

## 38. What HGY Is Ultimately Trying to Establish

HGY is trying to establish a cultural idea:

**Personal development deserves a practice.**

People already understand that physical skill, sport, music, language, rehabilitation, and professional mastery require repeated practice.

The Have a Great Yesterday Method™ brings that same logic to inner development.

The practice is deliberately small enough to sustain, meaningful enough to matter, structured enough to repeat, and continuous enough to create visible evidence.

That evidence supports a more accurate relationship with oneself and one’s life.

The destination is:

**Clarity. Accuracy. Self-Confidence.**

# Known Open Questions / Not Yet Locked

1. **Formal step architecture.**  
   Awareness → Notice → Capture → Interpret → Accumulate → Continue is useful and strongly aligned with the Method, but it has not been formally locked as the permanent official step sequence.

2. **Exact role of Resilience Horizon inside HGY.**  
   It belongs to the wider MyDopa architecture, but its precise conceptual relationship to HGY still needs fuller definition.

3. **Exact role and final definition of Praxis.**  
   Praxis is clearly aligned with practice/application, but the product and Method relationship can be developed further.

4. **Scientific validation of the complete Method.**  
   HGY currently synthesizes mechanisms supported by adjacent research. The complete Method itself has not yet been directly tested as a formal intervention.

5. **Professional framework architecture.**  
   The language and research territories are clear, but a formal professional model or practitioner framework has not yet been locked.

6. **Formal visual system.**  
   HGY + V + C = C, A & S-C and the staircase/kaizen visual are useful established frameworks, but the final visual language of the Method can continue to evolve.

7. **Exact boundaries between HGY and broader MyDopa doctrine.**  
   The Method/product distinction is clear, though some messaging territories remain shared and will need careful editorial management.

8. **Book architecture.**  
   HGY is intended to become René’s first book, but the chapter structure, narrative order, research architecture, and final book thesis are not yet locked.

9. **Research hierarchy expansion.**  
   Additional books, authors, papers, and historical sources will continue to be added. Their relevance should be assessed through the HGY research lens before being promoted from secondary material to foundational status.

10. **Terminology around “habit.”**  
    Habit science is clearly relevant, especially for professional content. Consumer-facing use remains intentionally selective so HGY and MyDopa do not collapse into the crowded “habit tracker” category.
